Section 03

Receptive Field Analysis

Theoretical formulas, effective gradient-based ERF maps, Gaussian sigma fitting, and remote sensing implications.

Theoretical Receptive Field

CNN Formula

For $L$ layers with kernel size $k_i$ and stride $s_i$:

$$\text{RF}_L = 1 + \sum_{i=1}^{L}(k_i - 1)\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}s_j$$

For our SmallCNN ($k=3$, $s=1$ throughout, $L=4$):

$$\text{RF}_4 = 1 + 4 \times (3-1) \times 1 = 9 \text{ px}$$

ViT: Global from Layer 1

Self-attention is all-pairs: every patch can attend to every other patch. The theoretical RF is the entire image from the very first attention layer:

$$\text{RF}_\text{ViT} = H \times W = 64 \times 64 = 4096 \text{ px}$$

Mamba: Linear Causal Growth

The SSM processes the sequence causally. At layer $l$, each token can integrate all prior tokens, but the reach in pixel-space grows with the patch size:

$$\text{RF}_\text{Mamba} \approx \min\!\left(L_\text{layers} \times p^2 \times \alpha,\; H \times W\right)$$

where $p=4$ is the patch size and $\alpha$ is an empirical growth factor.

⚠ Remote Sensing Implication
A CNN with RF=9 on a 512-pixel image means each neuron sees only $9/512 = 1.76\%$ of the image width. For flood mapping or land-cover classification requiring global context, this is catastrophic.

Effective Receptive Field (ERF)

Luo et al. (2017) showed that the gradient magnitude at the input follows a 2-D Gaussian distribution centred on the output pixel. Pixels far from the centre contribute exponentially less — the effective RF is much smaller than the theoretical RF.

Measurement Method

  1. Create a random input $x \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 3 \times 64 \times 64}$
  2. Forward pass → get centre-pixel output (or centre token for ViT/Mamba)
  3. Backpropagate: $\nabla_x \, \text{output}$
  4. ERF $= |\nabla_x|$ summed over channels, normalised to $[0,1]$
  5. Fit 2-D Gaussian → report $\sigma$

Remote Sensing Implications

CNN

RF=9px on a 512px image sees only 1.7% of the scene per neuron. Good for local texture tasks (road extraction, building detection) where global context is not required. Fails on land-use classification of large homogeneous regions.

ViT

Global RF from layer 1 enables scene-level understanding. Every patch can directly compare with every other patch. Ideal for global land cover, but expensive ($O(N^2)$) for large tiles.

Mamba

Linear causal RF grows with sequence length at $O(N)$ cost. The causal design means earlier patches in the scan order have more influence — a potential bias for row-major image processing.
