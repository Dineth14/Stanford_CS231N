Frequency Response Analysis

Fourier analysis of feature maps, spectral response heatmaps, and high vs low frequency alignment.

Spatial Frequency Primer

Spatial frequency in images refers to how rapidly pixel intensities change across space:

Low Frequencies

Smooth gradients, large-scale structure, overall illumination, scene layout. Dominant in optical satellite images of vegetation and water.

High Frequencies

Sharp edges, fine texture, speckle noise. Dominant in SAR imagery. Also encodes boundary information critical for segmentation.

The 2-D Discrete Fourier Transform of a feature map $f$ is:

$$F[u,v] = \sum_{x=0}^{H-1}\sum_{y=0}^{W-1} f[x,y]\, e^{-2\pi i(ux/H + vy/W)}$$

The power spectrum $|F[u,v]|^2$ shows how much energy the model assigns to each spatial frequency.

CNN as a Bank of Bandpass Filters

By the convolution theorem:

$$\mathcal{F}[f * g] = \mathcal{F}[f] \cdot \mathcal{F}[g]$$

Each convolutional filter $g$ has its own frequency response $\mathcal{F}[g]$. The output feature map is the input spectrum multiplied by the filter's frequency response. A Gaussian filter, for instance, is a low-pass filter with Gaussian frequency response. A Sobel edge detector is a high-pass filter.

Learned CNN filters tend to be oriented bandpass filters — similar to Gabor wavelets — concentrating energy in specific frequency bands and orientations. This is why CNNs are said to have a high-frequency bias when trained on texture-rich datasets.

Remote Sensing Implications

CNN

Natural high-frequency bias. Excellent for SAR texture analysis and road/building edge detection. Struggles with smooth low-frequency land cover gradients.

ViT

No inherent frequency bias — learns from data. With ImageNet pretraining, tends toward low-frequency global structure. Adaptable but data-hungry.

Mamba

SSM = IIR filter with smooth frequency rolloff. Naturally handles long-range low-frequency dependencies. Well-suited for hyperspectral sequence analysis.

💡 Change Detection Recommendation
Change detection requires both local high-frequency edge information (texture changes) and global low-frequency context (scene-level change). An ensemble of CNN + ViT, or a hybrid CNN-Mamba, is recommended over a single architecture.
